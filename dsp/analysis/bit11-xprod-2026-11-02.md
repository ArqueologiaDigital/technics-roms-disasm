## 1. Why a cross-product alignment, and how it was scored

Every earlier bit-11 census counted words within a corpus. This one asks the homolog pairs, the
same object `xprod_homolog.py` uses for the relocation test, a different question: what does the
other product's copy put at the aligned slot?

* Pairs are matched by effect name: 32 pairs, `rock_rotary` <-> `rotary_speaker`. The kernel, the
  epilogue and the `struct_*` images are left out.
* The alignment is `difflib.SequenceMatcher` over whole 36-bit words, with autojunk off. A word
  inside an `equal` block is IDENTICAL. Inside a non-equal block it is SWAPPED if the other side
  holds the same `lo12` on another carrier, DROPPED if the other image has that `lo12` nowhere,
  and DISPLACED otherwise.
* The control is printed first: the same classification for every decoded, non-bit-11 word whose
  `(hi12, class4, lo12)` form occurs once in its image. That is the population an `8BC` drop has
  to be read against (item D).
* Rule 9: images are de-duplicated (`class_twins.images()`, as `bit11_prize.py` does).
* The fate table counts each occurrence on **both** sides, so an aligned pair contributes 2 to
  IDENTICAL. The relocation table counts **pairs**.

## 2. What the swaps and the drops say, and what they do not

The four carrier swaps (item C) are the controlled form of what `SPECULATIVE-APPLIED-REGISTER.md`
§183 found from co-occurrence: the same `8BC` operation is in the same program, at the same slot,
on a bare word in one product and fused into a delay read in the other. Carrier independence used
to be inferred from two *different* images; here it is one program in two products.

[INFERENCE] The WSA copies of those four programs therefore carry one more delay read at `w0` than
the KN copies. That is checkable from the descriptor counts. It was **not** checked here: the WSA
records' fields `+0`/`+8` carry no tag-`0x4C` descriptor pokes (de-framed with
`wsa1/dsp/analysis/dsp_cram_map.deframe_stateful`: 0 cells in all 48 records), so the WSA descriptor
values must come from somewhere this tree has not parsed yet. Next step if anyone wants it: find the
WSA1R's runtime descriptor writer (the counterpart of the KN5000's `T_PARAM` streams).

The two drops (MIX UP, S.DELAY+CHORUS) are where a reader would like to conclude "the KN5000 runs
these programs without `8BC`, so `8BC` is optional". Item D is why this note does not: the control
drops 7.4 % of ordinary once-per-image words, and the identical run after each drop is short (1
and 3 words), so these are revised programs, not one program with one word inserted.

## 3. The relocation test on the alternate encoding

`xprod_homolog.py --reloc` runs the relocation test only on C-format pairs, and reported "no
comparable C-format pair in the divergences" for both pairs above its floor. The bit-11 words
need no floor: every bit-11-bearing KN5000 body loads at I-RAM 84 (the `.dsm` headers), every
SX-WSA1R body at 110 (`wsa1/dsp/README.md`), so every aligned bit-11 word has moved by 26 words in
I-RAM.

```
   lo12   aligned-identical   moved in I-RAM   moved within the body
   864                    1                1                       0
   8BC                   16               16                       0
   921                    1                1                       0
   C63                   44               44                      41
```

All 62 are byte-identical. **The alternate `lo12` (and `addr8`, and `hi12`) of these words holds no
I-RAM address that moves with the body.** This is the §122 method, and it gives the opposite answer
here. It is worth zero words: no word was decodable for want of this fact. Not covered: an address
into a space both products use the same way (C-RAM, the register file, D-RAM, the descriptor
space), which a relocation of I-RAM cannot move.

## 4. The `--dram` migration census

Same alignment, on the delay-DRAM escape words. For each `replace` block it pairs DRAM words on the
same carrier (`hi12`, `class4`, `addr8`) whose `lo12` differs, and checks whether the `lo12` that
left reappears on a non-DRAM neighbour (two words either side) in the other copy. Whole tally in the
data file. The one exceptionless row is item G: `407` on the KN read, `00B` on the WSA read, and the
`407` route next door in the WSA copy, in 6 of 6.

What G gives `ACT 0x0B` is item H, and only that. It does **not** show that `00B` is a filler or a
no-op. `000` is not a no-op (`ACT 0x00 = acc input term <- bus`, `dsp_disasm.LO_ACT_ACC_BUS`), and
the anchored no-op ACTION `0x15` (`LO_ACT_NONE_5`) is common on delay words (`2D5` x109, `1D5` x72,
`655` x16, `415` x1, same census). So the WSA authors had a no-op `lo12` available and wrote `00B`.
[INFERENCE, weak] That leans against `ACT 0x0B = none` at those reads; it is not evidence, because
`00B` may simply be the assembler's spelling of a plain read (it is on 42 `880.1.30` reads).

corpus that varies.

## 6. What this means for the reverb (`kn5000-i2m`)

Item I. The micro-topology question in [`reverb-topology-round7.md`](reverb-topology-round7.md) §12
is blocked on BLOCK B's words, the read latency `land` (the pipelined-read pool matches nothing), the
head / pre-delay loop, and the write-data source. Of those, the words the reverb still cannot execute
are the nine of item I. None is a bit-11 word. The decode-status page's R6 already names the exact
read latency inside its window as unobtainable from either board's software. And the SX-WSA1R's
reverb is no homolog: 21 of 133 words align, and it has 40 delay consumers against the KN5000's 32
cells. So the only thing that can choose between the pipe-comb and the series-comb cascade is the
real KN5000's reverb response: an impulse recorded at EFFECT OUT on ROOM REVERB 1. That is
`BLOCKED` on `kn5000-hardware-inaccessible`.
